# POS order taking

The POS is where most of service happens, so it’s built for speed and muscle memory. Big tappable
targets, sensible defaults, and nothing between you and sending the order.

## Start an order

Pick a table from the [floor plan](/docs/tables-floor-plan) or start a quick counter order. Tap a
category, tap an item, and it lands on the ticket. Adjust quantities inline.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose a table or counter">
    Table orders attach to the floor plan; counter orders are for takeaway and quick service.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add items and modifiers">
    Tap to add. If an item has modifiers, you’ll be prompted — or skip straight through for the
    defaults.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send to the kitchen">
    Hit **Send** and the ticket appears instantly on the [Kitchen Display](/docs/kitchen-display).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Courses and timing

Group items into courses so the kitchen fires them in the right order. Hold a course and release it
when the table’s ready — the line sees exactly when to start.

<Callout tone="tip" title="Fire on your terms">
  Sending the ticket and firing a course are separate actions. Send the whole order up front, then
  release each course when the table is ready for it.
</Callout>

## Splitting and merging checks

Service is messy, so the POS makes it easy to:

- **Split** a check by guest, by item, or evenly
- **Merge** checks when parties combine
- **Transfer** items or whole checks between tables

Every change keeps the kitchen and reporting in sync.

## Payments

Take payment at the table or counter. Restaurant OS records the tender type and updates your
[reports](/docs/reports-dashboard) in real time. Payment processing fees are passed through at cost.

<Note>
  The POS works offline. If your connection drops mid-service, orders queue locally and sync the
  moment you’re back online.
</Note>
